## Motor Schemas Controller
This controller is built on the motor schemas architecture, a concurrent and distributed model in which each behavior is represented as a vector and coordination is achieved purely through vector addition. Unlike subsumption, there is no predefined hierarchy: all schemas contribute simultaneously to the robot's motion, and the final movement emerges from their sum. This makes the approach more fluid, as behaviors blend into each other rather than one completely overriding another.

The computation follows two levels of aggregation. First, within each behavior, every individual sensor has its own perceptual schema that produces a vector. These are summed across the sensor group to produce a single behavior vector. Then, all behavior vectors are summed together to get the final motion vector, which is translated into differential wheel speeds through a roto-translational to differential steering conversion.

**Wander** uses a uniform field and needs no perceptual schema, since the force is not driven by any environmental stimulus. Each step, the vector is assigned a randomly sampled length between 0.5 and 1.0, with angle fixed at 0 (straight ahead). The random magnitude is a deliberate choice: a fixed wander would produce a constant forward bias, but when attractive and repulsive forces nearly cancel each other out, a varying wander weight perturbs the sum differently each step and nudges the robot out of the local minimum.

**Attractive (phototaxis)** uses an attractive potential field toward the light. Each of the 24 light sensors has its own perceptual schema producing a vector pointing toward that sensor with length equal to its reading. These are summed across all sensors into a single vector pointing in the aggregate direction of the light source, then normalized by the total intensity to keep the magnitude bounded and comparable with the other schemas.

**Repulsive (obstacle avoidance)** follows the same structure. Each of the 24 proximity sensors has its own perceptual schema producing a vector pointing away from that sensor. The direction is flipped using a standard angle negation, adding pi to the original angle and normalizing between -pi and +pi. These are summed across all sensors and normalized by total proximity intensity. A weight factor is also applied to give the repulsive schema stronger influence, ensuring that obstacle avoidance can effectively overcome the attractive and wander contributions when the robot is close to an object.

The motor schemas controller is simple and easy to implement, but it is not optimized for reaching the light because it lacks a more controlled strategy that can explicitly plan a path around obstacles instead of relying purely on the instantaneous balance of competing force vectors. In this specific case, noise (both from sensors and actuators) can also help the robot escape local minima by perturbing the summed vector enough to break the symmetry between competing behaviors.
